You can order a whole night at an izakaya with about ten short Japanese phrases: one to say how many you are, one to call staff, a few to order drinks and food, and two to pay and say thank you. Each is listed below with Japanese script, romaji and a simple pronunciation hint. Staff in busy places are used to short, simple requests, so a friendly "sumimasen" and a pointed finger already go a long way.

Picture Ueno just after dark. Under the train tracks south of the station, red lanterns glow over small tables, and the clatter of the trains overhead mixes with the shouts of "irasshaimase!" (welcome!) as each new group walks in. The menu may be handwritten, the room is loud, and nobody will come to you unless you speak up.

That is the part that worries most first-time visitors. Here is how to handle it, one moment at a time.

**【A quick note on pronunciation】** Japanese syllables are short and even, without the strong stress English uses. Say each one clearly and at the same strength, and you will be understood. The hints below use English sounds as a rough guide.

## ① At the door: say how many, then call for help

![Izakaya seats under the train tracks in Ueno](/images/tours/ueno-ameyoko/story-3.webp)

When you walk in, staff will usually ask *nanmei-sama desu ka?* (How many people?). You answer with your number plus *desu*.

**【Phrase 1: Futari desu】** 二人です — "Two people."
Say it: *foo-tah-ree dess*. For one person it is *hitori desu* (*hee-toh-ree dess*), for three *san-nin desu* (*sahn-neen dess*). Holding up fingers at the same time is perfectly fine.

**【Phrase 2: Sumimasen!】** すみません — "Excuse me!"
Say it: *soo-mee-mah-sen*. This is the word you will use most. In an izakaya it is normal to raise your hand and call it out loud enough to be heard. Some tables have a call button instead (*yobidashi botan*), which does the same job.

## ② The first drink: "toriaezu nama" and a toast

Drinks come first at an izakaya. Most groups order a round while they look at the menu, and draft beer is the classic opener.

**【Phrase 3: Toriaezu nama de】** とりあえず生で — "A draft beer to start."
Say it: *toh-ree-ah-eh-zoo nah-mah deh*. *Toriaezu* means "for now" and *nama* means draft beer. To order a number, add it: *nama futatsu kudasai* (*nah-mah foo-tah-tsoo koo-dah-sigh*), "two draft beers, please."

**【Phrase 4: Kanpai!】** 乾杯 — "Cheers!"
Say it: *kahn-pie*. Wait until everyone has a glass, then raise yours together.

**【Counting things】** Japanese has a simple set of numbers for ordering items: *hitotsu* (one), *futatsu* (two), *mittsu* (three), *yottsu* (four). Put the number after the item and finish with *kudasai* or *onegaishimasu* (please).

## ③ Ordering food: point, ask, and mention allergies

![A seafood stall in Ameyoko](/images/tours/ueno-ameyoko/gallery-7.webp)

Izakaya food comes in small plates for sharing, and it is normal to order a few dishes, eat, and order more later rather than everything at once.

**【Phrase 5: Kore kudasai】** これください — "This one, please."
Say it: *koh-reh koo-dah-sigh*. Point at the menu, a photo, or the dish on the next table if you must. It is the most useful phrase when you cannot read the menu.

**【Phrase 6: Osusume wa nan desu ka?】** おすすめは何ですか — "What do you recommend?"
Say it: *oh-soo-soo-meh wah nahn dess kah*. Staff will usually point to the house favourite or something seasonal. Follow it with *sore kudasai* (*soh-reh koo-dah-sigh*), "that one, please."

**【Phrase 7: ___ arerugii ga arimasu】** ＿アレルギーがあります — "I have a ___ allergy."
Say it: *ah-reh-roo-gee gah ah-ree-mahss*. Put the allergen in front, for example *ebi* (shrimp) or *kurumi* (walnuts). Say it when you sit down, before anything arrives. For a serious allergy, carry a card written in Japanese, because a short conversation in a noisy room cannot rule out cross-contact.

**【About the small dish you did not order】** Many izakaya bring a small dish with your first drink and charge it per person. It is a normal seat charge called *otoshi*, explained in our [guide to otoshi and cover charges](/column/japanese-bar-cover-charge-otoshi).

## ④ Another round, the easy way

**【Phrase 8: Okawari onegaishimasu】** おかわりお願いします — "Another one, please."
Say it: *oh-kah-wah-ree oh-neh-guy-shee-mahss*. Lift your empty glass as you say it, and staff will bring the same drink again.

**【Tablets and QR codes】** Many chain izakaya now take orders on a tablet at the table or through a QR code you scan with your phone, sometimes with an English option. Smaller independent places still take orders by voice. If a tablet order seems stuck, ask the staff rather than tapping again.

## ⑤ Leaving: the bill and a thank-you

![A tiny bar entrance with a red lantern](/images/tours/goldengai-nightlife/gallery-7.webp)

**【Phrase 9: Okaikei onegaishimasu】** お会計お願いします — "The bill, please."
Say it: *oh-kye-keh oh-neh-guy-shee-mahss*. At many places you take the bill to the register near the entrance instead of paying at the table. If your group wants to split it, ask *betsubetsu ni dekimasu ka?* (*beh-tsoo-beh-tsoo nee deh-kee-mahss kah*), "can we pay separately?"

**【Phrase 10: Gochisousama deshita】** ごちそうさまでした — "Thank you for the meal."
Say it: *go-chee-soh-sah-mah desh-tah*. Say it on your way out. Staff often answer warmly, and it is a nice way to end the night. There is no need to tip.

**【The same lines work in tiny bars】** Small bars, like the ones in Shinjuku's Golden Gai, often add a cover charge, so ask *chāji wa ikura desu ka?* (how much is the charge?) before you sit.
