Shinjuku, Tokyo—the world's busiest transport hub. While this area bustles as a shopping and business district during the day, it transforms into one of the world's premier nightlife Meccas as soon as the sun goes down.

The greatest appeal of Shinjuku's nightlife lies in its overwhelming "diversity." Nestled at the foot of towering skyscrapers are uniquely captivating drinking streets—ranging from narrow alleys lined with red paper lanterns that strongly evoke post-WWII Showa-era nostalgia, to the latest indoor entertainment food halls glowing with cyberpunk neon lights.

In this first installment of our series for travelers looking to experience Tokyo's deep nightlife, we are introducing 7 must-visit drinking alleys and *Yokocho* (alleyways) in the Shinjuku area!

## ① Shinjuku West Exit Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane)

![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/01.webp)![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/02.webp)

**【What Kind of Place is It?】** With roots tracing back to the post-WWII black markets of the late 1940s, this legendary alleyway is affectionately known to international travelers as "Memory Lane" or "Piss Alley." Packed tightly into a narrow lane along the train tracks are roughly 70 tiny Izakayas, yakitori (grilled chicken) stalls, and ramen shops hanging red paper lanterns.

**🌏 Tourist Difficulty Level: ★★☆☆☆ (Relatively Easy to Enter)**

> **💡 Pro-Tip** With the recent surge in foreign tourists, an increasing number of shops now offer English menus! Even if you don't step inside for a drink, simply walking through the smoke-filled alleyway and snapping a few photos will give you the ultimate retro cyberpunk experience.

## ② Shinjuku Golden Gai

![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/03.webp)![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/04.webp)

**【What Kind of Place is It?】** Located in a corner of the sleepless district of Kabukicho, this is an otherworldly area lined with low-rise wooden buildings. Once a secret salon where Japanese authors, film directors, musicians, and other cultural figures gathered nightly for heated debates, it has now evolved into a global bar hopping hotspot visited by travelers from all over the world.

**🌏 Tourist Difficulty Level: ★★★☆☆ (Intermediate - Beware of Local Rules)**

> **💡 Pro-Tip** Most bars here are so tiny they fill up with just a handful of people! Since many places require a "cover charge" (seating fee), be sure to check the signs at the entrance carefully. Choosing bars with signs that say "No Charge" or "English Menu Available" is a safe bet. With close proximity to the regulars, it's the perfect spot for anyone looking to mingle with locals and international travelers!

## ③ Shinjuku Center Gai - Omoide no Nukemichi (Memory Passage)

![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/05.webp)![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/06.webp)

**【What Kind of Place is It?】** While "Omoide Yokocho" and "Golden Gai" have gained worldwide fame and become major tourist spots, "Omoide no Nukemichi" is the rare gem that still strongly retains a local, underground, and somewhat untouched vibe. Hidden quietly in the gaps of the neon city, this narrow alley is lined with small bars and Izakayas run by uniquely charismatic owners.

**🌏 Tourist Difficulty Level: ★★★★★ (Advanced - Super Deep & Local!)**

> **💡 Pro-Tip** Its mysterious atmosphere might feel a bit intimidating for first-timers, but step inside, and you'll experience Tokyo's true, deep subculture. This is a secluded hideaway recommended for adventurous travelers wanting a peek into the real, non-touristy underground of Japan.

## ④ Shinjuku 3-Chome (Suehiro-dori & Donzoko-dori Area)

![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/07.webp)![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/08.webp)

**【What Kind of Place is It?】** Rather than catering primarily to tourists, this is an authentic gourmet area where Tokyo office workers and local youth gather every night. Centered around the historic traditional comedy theater "Shinjuku Suehirotei," the stone-paved streets are overflowing with countless restaurants and drinking spots.

**🌏 Tourist Difficulty Level: ★★☆☆☆ (Great for Food-Focused Fun & Easy to Enjoy)**

> **💡 Pro-Tip** With plenty of open-air Izakayas and bistros offering extensive menus, this is the ideal area for anyone wanting to savor delicious Japanese "Izakaya" gourmet! Grab a seat on a terrace spilling out onto the street, raise your glass with local office workers, and you'll feel like a true Tokyoite in no time.

## ⑤ Hobo Shinjuku Norengai

![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/09.webp)![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/10.webp)

**【What Kind of Place is It?】** As the name "Hobo Shinjuku" (Almost Shinjuku) suggests, this is a new style of Yokocho created by completely renovating a cluster of traditional old Japanese houses right behind Yoyogi Station. It beautifully preserves the traditional aesthetics of old Japan while offering a modern, clean, and comfortable drinking space.

**🌏 Tourist Difficulty Level: ★☆☆☆☆ (Very Easy to Enter & Super Comfortable)**

> **💡 Pro-Tip** Though the buildings are historic, the interiors have been modernly renovated, meaning everything is super clean—including the restrooms! It is a stress-free spot for female travelers, couples, or anyone who might feel a bit intimidated by gritty, old-school alleys. Plus, the Japanese-modern aesthetics make for fantastic photos!

## ⑥ Shinjuku Kabuki Hall - Kabukicho Yokocho

![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/11.webp)![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/12.webp)

**【What Kind of Place is It?】** Located on the 2nd floor of the skyscraper "Tokyu Kabukicho Tower" (opened in 2023), this is a next-generation entertainment food hall featuring roughly 1,000 seats. Themed around traditional Japanese "Matsuri" (festivals), the space is brilliantly decorated with glowing lanterns, neon lights, and massive screens.

**🌏 Tourist Difficulty Level: ★☆☆☆☆ (A Welcoming Entertainment Space for Everyone)**

> **💡 Pro-Tip** Set up like a spacious indoor food court, the ordering system is simple and easy for anyone to enjoy. The biggest perk is that you can taste regional soul food from all over Japan in one place! If you want to enjoy DJ music and a lively, festival-like atmosphere without worrying about the rain, this is the spot for you.

## ⑦ Ryuno-to Shinjuku Higashiguchi Yokocho (Dragon City Drinking Street)

![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/13.webp)![](/images/columns/shinjuku-yokocho-guide/14.webp)

**【What Kind of Place is It?】** Suddenly appearing underground right next to Shinjuku Station's East Exit, this is an otherworldly neo-drinking alley bathed in vivid neon lights. It has become a hot topic for its overwhelming photogenic space, which looks as if a "cyberpunk near-future Asian metropolis" from a Japanese anime has been brought to life.

**🌏 Tourist Difficulty Level: ★☆☆☆☆ (Enjoyable Like a Theme Park)**

> **💡 Pro-Tip** It feels just like a neon-lit theme park! This is a must-visit spot if you're aiming for amazing photos for your social media. Because it’s underground, weather is never an issue. With street food stalls offering various cuisines from across Asia, it's perfect for groups looking to share and sample a wide variety of dishes!
