In our previous article, we introduced all the recommended drinking alleyways (*Yokocho*) in Shinjuku. This time, we are diving deep into the most underground and local spot of them all: **Shinjuku Center Gai (commonly known as "Omoide no Nukemichi" / Memory Passage)**!

While "Golden Gai" and "Omoide Yokocho" are bustling with international travelers every day as globally famous tourist spots, Omoide no Nukemichi is a rare gem that still strongly retains a real, untouchable atmosphere—as if time stopped in Showa-era Kabukicho.

It might feel a bit intimidating for first-timers, but once you know the rules and take a step inside, the "real Tokyo nightlife" awaits. For adventurers seeking the ultimate local experience, here are the tips on how to navigate it and our top recommended shops!

## 🏮 What Kind of Place is "Omoide no Nukemichi"?

![](/images/columns/shinjuku-center-gai-guide/02.webp)

- **Location:** 1-chome, Kabukicho, Shinjuku City, Tokyo (Right behind the Shinjuku City Office)
- **Access:** Approx. 6-minute walk from JR Shinjuku Station East Exit

While its official name is "Shinjuku Center Gai," it is affectionately known by the nostalgic neon sign at its entrance reading **"Omoide no Nukemichi"** (Memory Passage).

Packed into this narrow, dimly lit alley are small snack bars, deep dive bars, and international eateries. Far from a neatly polished tourist attraction, it features bare concrete, casually stacked beer crates, and mysteriously glowing signs. For travelers seeking a cyberpunk vibe or Japanese nostalgia, simply walking through is an overwhelmingly photogenic experience that will give you goosebumps.

From delicious food to deep bar-hopping, we’ve selected four amazing spots where you can fully enjoy this alleyway.

## 🔰 3 Rules Every International Traveler Should Know

This alleyway is not a tourist spot, but a local place where regular patrons come to quietly enjoy their drinks. To ensure a fun and safe night out, keep the following rules in mind.

### Rule 1: We Recommend Visiting with a Japanese Friend!

While Omoide no Nukemichi's old-school, deep atmosphere is its biggest charm, most shops are not customized for inbound tourists and do not have English menus available. You might encounter a language barrier, so if you want to enjoy a local Japanese night out with peace of mind, exploring with a Japanese friend is the absolute best way to go!

### Rule 2: Prepare for "Cover Charges" and Bring "Cash"

Most bars charge a customary Japanese "cover charge" (seating/table fee, known as *charge* or *otoshi*), which typically ranges from 500 to 1,000 yen. Many shops do not accept credit cards, so be sure to bring **Cash**. If the pricing system is not displayed on the door, it's a good idea to confirm the cover charge with the bartender when you enter.

### Rule 3: Always Ask the Bartender Before Taking Photos Inside

While taking photos of the alley's exterior is fine, pointing your camera around inside a bar without permission is considered bad manners. Many regular patrons dislike having their faces photographed, so if you want to take a picture inside, always ask the bartender or owner first: "Can I take a picture?"
