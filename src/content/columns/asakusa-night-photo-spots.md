If you want to capture the perfect memorial photo in Asakusa, the real magic happens after the sun goes down. Once the daytime hustle and bustle fades, nighttime Asakusa transforms into a massive photo studio where you can capture "miracle shots" completely free of other tourists.

Here is a definitive list of nighttime photo spots in Asakusa that are currently gaining immense popularity among international travelers on platforms like Instagram and TikTok.

## 7 Must-Visit Instagrammable Spots in Asakusa at Night

**1. The "Looking Up" Angle Directly Under Kaminarimon Gate**

![](/images/columns/asakusa-night-photo-spots/02.webp)

Nearly impossible to photograph cleanly during the day, Kaminarimon is all yours at night. Stepping directly under the giant lantern and pointing your camera straight up is a massive trend on Instagram. A professional composition captures the intricate carving of the water dragon—a deity protecting the wooden gate from fire—at the bottom of the lantern, along with its glowing bamboo frame.

**2. Nakamise Street Shutter Art (Asakusa Emaki)**

![](/images/columns/asakusa-night-photo-spots/03.webp)

This 250-meter-long gallery only appears after 7:00 PM. The shutters of every closed shop reveal beautiful paintings of Edo-period history and festivals. A composition featuring this straight, illuminated scroll of street art leading up to the glowing Hozomon Gate is currently going viral on social media.

**3. Senso-ji Hozomon Gate & Giant Straw Sandals**

A massive gate located at the end of Nakamise Street. The illuminated architecture and the 4.5-meter-tall *O-waraji* (giant straw sandals) hung on its back to ward off evil are breathtaking. A popular "frame within a frame" composition among photography enthusiasts involves using the gate's pillars to frame the glowing Main Hall or Five-Storied Pagoda in the background.

**4. Senso-ji Main Hall**

![](/images/columns/asakusa-night-photo-spots/04.webp)

While it’s difficult to capture the entire hall during the day due to the massive crowds, it falls into a deep silence at night. The combination of the giant lantern reading "Shinbashi" floating in the pitch-black sky and the silhouette of the massive illuminated roof is the very essence of traditional Japanese beauty.

**5. Senso-ji Five-Storied Pagoda**

Representing the Buddhist view of the universe, this pagoda is essentially a tomb for Buddha's ashes. Taking a low-angle shot from near the Main Hall, looking up at the sacred, vermilion tower glowing in the dark, creates a photo with incredible depth and overwhelming impact.

**6. Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center (8F Observation Terrace)**

![](/images/columns/asakusa-night-photo-spots/05.webp)

A hidden gem with a spectacular view, open for free until 10:00 PM. The view looking down over Asakusa at night is highly praised on Instagram, making the brilliantly illuminated, straight line of Nakamise Street and the entire Senso-ji complex look like a glowing miniature diorama.

**7. Hoppy Street**

![](/images/columns/asakusa-night-photo-spots/06.webp)

A sharp contrast to the silence of the temple, this Izakaya street overflows with local energy. The outdoor seating made of stacked beer crates and the warm glow of countless red lanterns make for the perfect subject to capture the "deep, authentic vibe of Japanese nightlife."

> **💡 Family Highlight / One-Point Guide: Smartphone Night Photography Tricks**

- **Puddle Reflections**: A rainy night is actually your best chance. Use the puddles on the temple's stone paving by holding your smartphone upside down, close to the water’s surface. This allows the Five-Storied Pagoda or Hozomon Gate to reflect in the water, creating a stunningly symmetrical and magical photo.
- **Keep the Flash OFF**: The temple's illuminations provide plenty of light. Using a flash will overexpose the subject and ruin the deep shadows. Stick to your camera's night mode for the best results.
