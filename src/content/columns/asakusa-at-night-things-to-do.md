Asakusa at night is quieter than by day, but plenty is still open after 5 PM. The Senso-ji grounds stay open and are lit up until late, Nakamise's closed shutters turn into a long painted scroll, a free rooftop deck looks out over it all, and Hoppy Street and the riverside keep going into the evening. What closes are the temple's Main Hall, most shops and the Kappabashi kitchen street.

Come around sunset and you can watch the change happen. The tour groups thin out, stall keepers pull down their shutters, and the lanterns of Kaminarimon start to glow. By the time it is fully dark, the vermilion of the Hozomon gate and the five-story pagoda stands out against the sky, and you can hear your own footsteps on the stone path.

It is the same Asakusa, just slower, and the streets to the west fill with the smoke and chatter of small izakaya.

## ① What closes at 5 PM, and what stays open

Before planning your evening, it helps to know where the line falls. These times come from travel listings and can change, so check each official site before you go.

| Place | In the evening |
|---|---|
| Senso-ji grounds | Open, free, lit up until about 11 PM |
| Senso-ji Main Hall | Doors close at 5:00 PM |
| Nakamise stalls | Most close in the early evening |
| Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center rooftop | Open into the evening, free |
| Hoppy Street izakaya | Many open until about 9-11 PM |
| Kappabashi kitchen shops | Most close around 5 PM |

**【The short version】** Do your shopping and anything indoors in the daytime. Keep the evening for walking, looking up and eating.

## ② Senso-ji after dark: the grounds are yours

![The lit-up Hozomon gate and five-story pagoda of Senso-ji at night](/images/tours/asakusa-night-photo-walk/story-3.webp)

**【What you can do】** The temple grounds have no gates that lock, so you can walk in at any hour. The Main Hall doors are shut after 5:00 PM, but the hall, Hozomon gate and five-story pagoda are lit up, and many travel guides say the lights stay on until about 11 PM. The temple has lit its buildings every evening for years, so you do not need to time your visit to a special event.

**【Why it feels different】** Without the daytime crowds, you can stand in the middle of the approach and take in the whole view: the giant lanterns, the straw sandals hanging on Hozomon and the pagoda behind. It is also a calmer time to pause and pay your respects from in front of the hall.

**【Please remember】** It is a working temple. Keep your voice down, and do not climb on anything for a photo.

## ③ Nakamise after closing: a street-long painted scroll

![Nakamise shopping street at night with its shutters down](/images/tours/asakusa-night-photo-walk/story-2.webp)

**【The art behind the shutters】** Nakamise's stalls are run independently, so there is no single closing time, but most are shut by the early evening. When the shutters come down, they show painted scenes known as Asakusa Emaki, or picture scrolls: festivals such as the Sanja Matsuri, seasonal events and moments from Asakusa's history. Sources differ on exactly when they were painted, but all place them around 1990.

**【How to enjoy it】** Walk slowly from Kaminarimon toward Hozomon and look at the shutters one by one, as if reading a scroll. The street stays open to walk through even after every shop is closed.

## ④ A free view from above, and a night at the theatre

**【The rooftop across from Kaminarimon】** The Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center, directly opposite Kaminarimon, has a free observation deck on the 8th floor. From there you look straight down Nakamise to Senso-ji, with Tokyo Skytree to the east. Listings say the deck stays open into the evening, later than the information desk below. Check the current hours with the center.

**【Rakugo at Asakusa Engei Hall】** If you would like to sit down for a while, Asakusa Engei Hall stages yose, Japan's traditional variety theatre, every day of the year: rakugo storytelling, manzai comedy, magic and paper-cutting. One guide lists an evening program that runs until around 8:30 PM. Tickets are sold at the door on the day, and you can walk in partway through. Most acts are in Japanese, so go for the atmosphere, the timing and the laughter of the room.

## ⑤ Eat and drink like a local: Hoppy Street and Denboin-dori

![Denboin-dori street in Asakusa glowing with lanterns in the evening](/images/tours/asakusa-night-photo-walk/story-5.webp)

**【Denboin-dori】** This short, pedestrian street west of Nakamise was restyled as an Edo-period shopping street in the early 2000s. In the evening its lanterns and wooden shop signs glow, and the daytime crowds are gone. It takes only a few minutes to walk end to end, and it leads you toward Hoppy Street.

**【Hoppy Street】** Named after Hoppy, a low-alcohol beer-flavored drink mixed with shochu, this lane is lined with small izakaya serving stewed beef tendon, skewers and cold drinks. Many have tables outside under the lanterns, even in winter. Each shop sets its own hours, and many close somewhere between about 9 and 11 PM, so do not leave dinner too late.

**【Tips】**
- Point at the menu or at what others are eating if there is no English menu.
- Some places may add a small seating charge. Ask before you order if you are unsure.

## ⑥ End by the river: Skytree and the Sumida

![Tokyo Skytree and the Sumida River at dusk](/images/tours/asakusa-night-photo-walk/gallery-6.webp)

**【Azuma Bridge and Sumida Park】** A few minutes' walk east of Kaminarimon, the Sumida River opens up. The red Azuma Bridge is lit at night and frames Tokyo Skytree across the water. Sumida Park, on the riverbank, is a good place to sit after dinner.

**【Sumida River Walk】** To cross on foot toward Skytree, take the Sumida River Walk, a pedestrian path running alongside the railway bridge. It is not open all night, so check its closing time before you head over.

**【What to skip at night】** Kappabashi, the kitchenware street west of Asakusa, is a daytime trip: most shops close around 5 PM and many close on Sundays.
