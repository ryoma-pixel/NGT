Japanese chef knives are highly coveted by cooking enthusiasts worldwide for their overwhelming sharpness and beauty. If you're looking for an authentic Japanese knife in Tokyo, there is no better place than "Kappabashi," a wholesale district home to about 170 specialty shops and wholesalers. However, when stepping into these professional-grade stores, many travelers feel anxious—worrying about the lack of English signs, having too many choices without knowing what to pick, or finding the artisan staff unapproachable. Here, we'll explain the basic rules so that even complete beginners with zero knowledge can confidently choose and score a lifelong Japanese knife at a great price.

## 1. The Ultimate Choice | Carbon Steel vs. Stainless Steel

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-japanese-knife-guide/01.webp)

**💡 Quick Tip: Why Do Japanese Knives Make Food Taste Better?**

> While most Western knives are "double-edged" (cutting from both sides), many traditional Japanese knives (like sashimi knives) are "single-edged" (sharpened to cleanly slice from one side). This allows you to cut through the cell walls of tomatoes or raw fish without crushing them. This keeps the moisture and umami locked in, dramatically improving the texture and flavor of the food.

## 2. Choose the "Shop Style" That Fits Your Vibe

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-japanese-knife-guide/02.webp)

Kappabashi has dozens of knife shops, but they can largely be divided into two distinct styles. Choosing a shop that matches your preference will dramatically lower the hurdle of shopping.

**[Style A: The Refined Gallery Type] (e.g., Kama-Asa Shoten)** These modern stores display knives beautifully like an art museum, complete with English spec signs and multilingual staff. They are very beginner-friendly, allowing you to take your time learning about the history and function of knives in a sophisticated space.

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-japanese-knife-guide/03.webp)![](/images/columns/kappabashi-japanese-knife-guide/04.webp)

- **Store Info**: Kama-Asa Shoten
- **Hours**: 10:00 AM – 5:30 PM (Open daily, except New Year holidays)

**[Style B: The Traditional Local Wholesaler Type] (e.g., Tsubaya)** Packed with thousands of exposed knives in a space so narrow that people can barely pass each other, these stores radiate an overwhelming artisan energy. English signage is scarce, but they are perfect for those who want to experience the local atmosphere of an "authentic artisan's secret base" frequented by professional chefs.

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-japanese-knife-guide/05.webp)![](/images/columns/kappabashi-japanese-knife-guide/06.webp)

- **Store Info**: Tsubaya
- **Hours**: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM (Open daily)

## 3. The Finishing Magic: Requesting the "Engraving" Service

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-japanese-knife-guide/07.webp)

Once you find your favorite knife, be sure to request "engraving" at checkout. Major knife shops in Kappabashi offer a service to hand-carve your name in Kanji or Katakana onto the blade of the knife you just bought (often for free or a small extra fee). Watching the craftsman use a chisel to engrave your name right in front of you is a fantastic piece of entertainment in itself. Don't miss the moment a simple kitchen tool transforms into a "one-of-a-kind personalized work of art."
