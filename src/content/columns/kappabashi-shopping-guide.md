The current historic weak yen is an incredible shopping opportunity for travelers visiting Japan. But there's no need to pay premium prices at high-end department stores in Ginza. If you want to score authentic "Made in Japan" goods smartly and affordably, head to Kappabashi—a wholesale district just a 10-minute walk from Asakusa. This is where Japanese professional chefs come to source their gear. It’s Tokyo’s ultimate secret spot for finding beautiful ceramics and unique kitchen tools at wholesale prices, with the middleman cut out.

## 1. A Treasure Hunt Starting at a Few Dollars| Digging for Ceramics at "Dengama"

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-shopping-guide/01.webp)

Located at the southern entrance of Kappabashi, "Dengama" is an absolute must-visit for traditional Japanese tableware. The two-story shop is packed floor to ceiling with classic regional pottery from across Japan, including Arita, Kutani, and Mino ware. The real highlight here is the wagons out front, piled high with small plates and chopstick rests. Starting at just a few hundred yen (a couple of dollars), these items easily slip into the gaps in your suitcase and won’t break your budget even if you buy several. Quality ceramics that would normally sit behind glass in a department store are casually stacked here, offering an immersive "treasure hunt" experience as you dig for your favorite patterns.

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-shopping-guide/02.webp)![](/images/columns/kappabashi-shopping-guide/03.webp)

**💡 Quick Tip: Clay (Earthenware) vs. Stone (Porcelain)**

> Japanese tableware generally falls into two categories. "Stone" (like Arita ware) is almost translucent white and makes a high-pitched ring when tapped. "Clay" (like Mino ware) has a warmer, thicker, and more textured feel. If you’re looking for durable, microwave-safe plates for everyday use, picking up small "stone" dishes is the smart choice.

- **Store Info**: Dengama
- **Hours**: 10:00 AM – 5:30 PM
- **Closed**: Open daily (except New Year holidays)

## 2. The Epic Cookie Cutter Tower | Artistic Baking Tools at "Majimaya Kiki"

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-shopping-guide/04.webp)

Once you’ve found your favorite ceramics, head a little further north up the street to "Majimaya Kiki." This historic shop specializes in molds and tools for both traditional Japanese and Western sweets. The moment you walk in, your eyes will be drawn to the stunning "Cookie Cutter Tower"—a towering wire mesh display featuring roughly 3,000 different cookie cutters. You can grab intricately designed cutters unique to Japan—like cherry blossoms, Mt. Fuji, origami cranes, and ninjas—for just a few hundred yen each. Since they are incredibly lightweight and unbreakable, there is no better affordable, thoughtful souvenir for friends back home who love baking.

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-shopping-guide/05.webp)

**💡 Quick Tip: Why Are They So Detailed?**

> Japan has a deep-rooted culture of making *nerikiri* (traditional Japanese sweets) crafted to reflect seasonal plants and animals. Tool shops like Majimaya have a long history of creating highly detailed wooden and metal molds to meet the exacting demands of these artisan confectioners. That master craftsmanship is applied to their modern cookie cutters today.

- **Store Info**: Majimaya Kiki
- **Hours**: Weekdays 9:00 AM – 5:30 PM / Weekends & Holidays 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM
- **Closed**: Irregular (usually closed over New Year's and summer holidays)
