### "Souvenir" vs. "Omiyage": What’s the Difference?

While "Omiyage" is usually translated as "souvenir" in English, the cultural meaning is completely different.

- **The Western "Souvenir"**  
  This is a keepsake bought for **yourself** to remember your trip. People often prefer long-lasting items like magnets, figurines, or t-shirts.
- **The Japanese "Omiyage"**  
  This is bought not for oneself, but as a **gift for others**—like coworkers, family, and friends back home. It is a communication tool used to share the joy of the trip and show appreciation to those who couldn't come.

Because of this, the most important feature of an Omiyage is how easy it is to share. This is why about 80% of Japanese souvenirs today are food items, with each cookie or cake individually and beautifully wrapped so they can be easily distributed at the office.

### The Roots of Omiyage in the Samurai Era

It is said that this culture took root among the general public during the Edo period (the era of the Samurai). Back then, traveling to famous shrines, like Ise Jingu, was a once-in-a-lifetime dream for ordinary people, but travel expenses were incredibly high. Therefore, villagers would pool their money together and send a representative. As proof of the journey and a token of gratitude, the traveler would bring back sacred charms and local specialties for the family and villagers who had funded the trip. This was the beginning of modern Omiyage culture.

### Top 3 Most Popular Japanese Souvenirs for Foreign Tourists

So, what do modern foreign tourists (especially from Western countries) buy when they visit Japan?

**#1: Matcha-Flavored Sweets (like Kit Kats)**

Matcha (green tea) sweets boast overwhelming popularity. Matcha-flavored Kit Kats, in particular, are highly sought after as a rare flavor not easily found in Western countries, making them a massive hit on social media and the perfect Omiyage to hand out.

**#2: Anime & Character Goods**

![](/images/columns/best-japan-souvenir-ranking/01.webp)

Japan’s world-famous pop culture! Figures and character goods of favorite anime series fill the suitcases of many travelers as enthusiastic personal "Souvenirs".

**#3: Traditional Crafts & Wares**

Items that perfectly combine traditional Japanese beauty with everyday practicality are highly praised as lifelong "Souvenirs."

### Spotlight: Top 3 "Traditional Items" for the Ultimate Lifelong Souvenir

**#1: Hanko (Personal Seals)**

![](/images/columns/best-japan-souvenir-ranking/02.webp)

You might be surprised, but articles about "Hanko" actually attract more page views than those about Kimono! As a unique cultural item not found in other countries, creating a personalized seal with your own name in Kanji has become the absolute number-one ultimate "Souvenir."

**#2: Japanese Knives (Hocho)**

Also surpassing Kimono in popularity, Japanese knives are highly praised worldwide for their incredible precision and sharpness. Not only high-end traditional crafts, but even standard knives sold in supermarkets are famous for their quality. Shopping for the perfect blade in places like Tokyo's Kappabashi Kitchen Town is a major trend.

**#3: Kimono & Yukata**

The classic, staple souvenirs for foreign tourists. Recently, rather than expensive new ones, affordable second-hand Kimono and Yukata have become particularly popular. Many travelers love hunting for these beautiful, budget-friendly garments to take home.
