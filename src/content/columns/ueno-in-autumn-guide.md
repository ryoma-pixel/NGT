Ueno in autumn is one of the easiest ways to see Tokyo's fall colours without leaving the city centre. From mid-November to early December, the ginkgo trees in Ueno Park turn gold and the maples turn red, and the big museums, Shinobazu Pond and the Ameyoko market are all within a short walk. With half a day and a free morning, you can see all of it.

Walk up from the station on a clear late-November morning and the park smells of fallen leaves. Yellow ginkgo leaves pile up along the paths, school groups queue outside the museums, and older locals sit on benches with cameras pointed at the trees. Fifteen minutes later, down by the tracks, the mood flips: shopkeepers are shouting prices and the fruit stalls are glowing.

That contrast is what makes Ueno such a good autumn day out. Here is where to look, what to plan around, and how to fit it into one easy half day.

## ① When to go: the short window for gold and red

**【Typical timing】** Travel guides and tourism sites usually give Ueno Park's best colour as mid-November to early December. Several point to late November as the peak. It changes from year to year with the weather, so check the current Tokyo autumn leaves forecast (GO TOKYO publishes one) a few days before you go.

**【What turns first?】** Ginkgo trees give the bright yellow, and Japanese maples give the red. Guides also mention zelkova and other trees adding orange and brown, so even a week early or late you will usually find some colour.

**【Best time of day】** Morning is calmer, and the low autumn sun makes the ginkgo glow. Days are short in late November. It gets dark around 5pm, so do not leave the park for last.

## ② Where to look in Ueno Park: ginkgo, maples and the pond

![A tunnel of red torii gates in Ueno Park](/images/tours/ueno-ameyoko/story-4.webp)

Ueno Park is big, but the autumn highlights sit along one easy loop.

1. **The Saigo Takamori statue.** Near the park's southern entrance, the bronze statue of Saigo Takamori and his dog stands in front of large ginkgo trees. Photographers like to frame the statue with the gold leaves behind it.
2. **Kiyomizu Kannon-do.** This small temple hall on the slope above the pond is surrounded by maples that guides describe as turning a deep red.
3. **The ginkgo path to the Tokyo National Museum.** The walk north through the park towards the museum is lined with ginkgo, and it is one of the most photographed stretches in autumn.
4. **Shinobazu Pond.** Walk the path around the pond and you see the trees reflected in the water. In summer the pond is covered in lotus. By late autumn the leaves are fading, which gives it a quieter, more Japanese-painting look.

**【Boats on the pond】** Rowboats and swan pedal boats are rented near Bentendo, the small temple on the island. Sources disagree about winter hours and current prices, so check on the day.

**【Ueno Toshogu】** The golden shrine to Tokugawa Ieyasu is a good stop on the same loop. Listings give its autumn and winter hours as 9:00 to 16:30, so visit it before the light goes.

![Ueno Toshogu Shrine](/images/tours/ueno-ameyoko/gallery-6.webp)

## ③ Museums: pick one, and check the closing day

Autumn is also a busy season for special exhibitions, and Ueno has some of Japan's biggest museums side by side.

| Museum | What it is | Usual closing day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tokyo National Museum | Japan's oldest museum, with Japanese art and artefacts | Mondays |
| National Museum of Western Art | European art in a Le Corbusier building, part of a UNESCO World Heritage listing | Mondays |
| Tokyo Metropolitan Art Museum | Large rotating exhibitions | 1st and 3rd Monday |

**【The Monday rule】** The national museums usually open on a Monday national holiday and close the next weekday instead. Special exhibitions sometimes keep their own hours, and many museums close over the New Year. Check each official site before you go.

**【Garden behind the Tokyo National Museum】** The museum has a Japanese garden with a pond, historic teahouses, and ginkgo and maple trees. Sources disagree on whether it is open all year or only in spring and autumn, so check the museum's site.

**【How much time?】** One museum fills a morning or an afternoon. Trying to do two big ones and the park in a single half day usually means rushing all three.

## ④ Market food: what to eat at Ameyoko in autumn

![Fruit stalls along Ameyoko](/images/tours/ueno-ameyoko/gallery-1.webp)

From the park, walk down the steps by the station and you are at Ameyoko, the market street along the tracks towards Okachimachi. Our [Ameyoko shopping guide](/column/ameyoko-shopping-guide-ueno) covers the street in detail. Here are the autumn notes.

**【Seasonal snacks】** Roasted sweet potato (*yaki-imo*) and roasted chestnuts (*yaki-guri*) are classic Tokyo snacks from autumn into winter. You will find them from trucks, stalls and shops around the city. Do not count on a particular stall, but keep an eye out.

**【Fruit stalls】** The fruit grocers on Ameyoko change their displays with the season, and cut fruit on a stick is an easy snack as you walk.

**【Warm up under the tracks】** Once the evening chill sets in, the small bars and izakaya under the elevated railway start to fill up. Our [guide to Ueno's izakaya](/column/ueno-izakaya-guide) explains how to order.

## ⑤ An easy half-day plan for Ueno in autumn

This plan starts in the morning and keeps the leaves first, while the light is good.

1. **Morning: the park loop.** Start at the Saigo statue, walk past Kiyomizu Kannon-do and down to Shinobazu Pond, then head back up to Toshogu.
2. **Late morning: one museum.** Take the ginkgo path to the Tokyo National Museum, or choose the Western Art or Metropolitan Art Museum. Skip this step on a Monday.
3. **Lunch: Ameyoko.** Walk down to the market for street food, fruit and snacks.
4. **Afternoon: a slow finish.** Browse the market or go back to the pond for the late-afternoon light before it gets dark.

**【Tips】**

- Wear comfortable shoes. The park has slopes and steps.
- Carry cash for market stalls.
- Bring a light jacket. Autumn evenings in Tokyo get cool fast.
