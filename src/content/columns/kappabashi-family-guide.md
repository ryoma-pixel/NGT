When visiting Tokyo with your family, Senso-ji Temple (Kaminarimon) in Asakusa is an absolute must-see, but the massive crowds can quickly exhaust young children. When that happens, Kappabashi—just a 10-minute walk away through quiet backstreets—is the perfect escape zone for families. While it’s famously Japan's largest wholesale district for professional kitchenware, it’s also a thrilling "Yokai" (spirit/monster) town for kids. While parents enjoy window-shopping for high-quality, Made-in-Japan goods, children can embark on an adventure to find "Kappa," the cute Yokai hiding throughout the neighborhood.

## 1. The Yokai That Saved the Town| The Kawataro Statue and the Kappa Legend

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-family-guide/01.webp)

In Japanese folklore, the "Kappa" is a mysterious, slightly comical Yokai that lives near water and has a dish-like depression on its head. About 200 years ago, this area suffered from poor drainage and frequent flooding. A local merchant named Kihachi Kappaya invested his own money to build a drainage canal. Legend has it that the Kappa living in the nearby Sumida River were so moved by his efforts that they came out night after night to help with the construction. Located in a small pocket park right in the middle of the shopping street is the golden "Kawataro Statue," a glowing symbol of this legend. Make this your first stop to greet the Yokai that saved the town.

- **Spot Info**: Kappabashi Kawataro Statue
- **Hours**: 24 hours (Outdoor monument)
- **Google Maps**: <https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=かっぱ河太郎像>

## 2. Kappa Hidden in Plain Sight | A Family Scavenger Hunt

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-family-guide/02.webp)

Once you’ve found the golden statue, the real scavenger hunt across the entire street begins! As you walk down the 800-meter main stretch of Kappabashi, you'll find different designs of Kappa hiding everywhere—on shop signs, sidewalk tiles, store shutters, and even manhole covers. By turning your walk into a game of "How many Kappa can you find by the next corner?", your kids will be completely absorbed in exploring the street without ever getting bored. Meanwhile, parents can take their time soaking in the unique sights of a traditional artisan district.

![](/images/columns/kappabashi-family-guide/03.webp)![](/images/columns/kappabashi-family-guide/04.webp)![](/images/columns/kappabashi-family-guide/05.webp)

**💡 Quick Tip for Families: Are Japanese "Yokai" Scary?**

> While Western monsters are often depicted as evil, Japanese Yokai are typically spirits residing in nature or old objects. They are charming creatures that might help humans or just play innocent pranks. The Kappa of Kappabashi are beloved by locals as "best friends" who saved the town, so they are definitely not something that will frighten your children!
