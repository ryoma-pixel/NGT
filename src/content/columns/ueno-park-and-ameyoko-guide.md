Ueno, where the direct express train (Keisei Skyliner) from Narita Airport arrives, serves as the "first gateway to Japan" for many international travelers. While famous as a cultural hub filled with massive parks and museums, the true charm of this town lies in the "extreme contrast" you can experience within just a few minutes' walk.

![](/images/columns/ueno-park-and-ameyoko-guide/01.webp)

Ueno is truly a microcosm of Tokyo, where traditional Japanese tranquility and energetic street culture exist back-to-back.

## ⛩️ The Tranquil Forest Built by the Tokugawa Shogunate (Ueno Park Area)

![](/images/columns/ueno-park-and-ameyoko-guide/02.webp)

The entire Ueno Park area today was once the grounds of "Kan'eiji Temple," a symbol of the Edo Shogunate's (Tokugawa family's) power. Built in the 17th century to protect Edo (present-day Tokyo) from evil spirits, the remnants of this massive temple complex can still be found deep within the forest today.

- **Hanazono Inari Shrine:** A hidden gem deep in the park, featuring a stunning row of vibrant red torii gates.

![](/images/columns/ueno-park-and-ameyoko-guide/03.webp)![](/images/columns/ueno-park-and-ameyoko-guide/04.webp)

- **Ueno Toshogu Shrine:** A magnificent, golden architectural masterpiece dedicated to the shogun Tokugawa Ieyasu.

![](/images/columns/ueno-park-and-ameyoko-guide/05.webp)![](/images/columns/ueno-park-and-ameyoko-guide/06.webp)

Walking through here, you'll feel such profound silence and the breathing presence of centuries-old samurai that you'll forget you're in the middle of a bustling metropolis.

## 🏮 The Chaos Born from Post-War Black Markets (Ameyoko & Under-Track Area)

On the other hand, a completely different world unfolds around the elevated railway tracks extending south from Ueno Station. This is "Ameyoko" and the red-lantern (*aka-chochin*) izakaya alley underneath the tracks.

- **The Roots of Ameyoko:** It originated as a spontaneous "Black Market" in the burnt-out ruins of Tokyo right after World War II. It is said to be called "Ameyoko" because many surplus American military goods were sold there.

![](/images/columns/ueno-park-and-ameyoko-guide/07.webp)![](/images/columns/ueno-park-and-ameyoko-guide/08.webp)

- **The Current Vibe:** A mix of cluttered signboards, the scent of Asian spices, and energetic street vendors shouting to sell their goods. The sight of local office workers sitting on upside-down beer crates and downing mugs of beer creates an overwhelming, vibrant energy.

![](/images/columns/ueno-park-and-ameyoko-guide/09.webp)![](/images/columns/ueno-park-and-ameyoko-guide/10.webp)

## 🔰 "Invisible Barriers" and Local Rules You Should Know

Strict historical landmarks and

deeply energetic back alleys. Ueno is the only place in Tokyo where you can hop between these two extremes in just a few minutes on foot. However, when diving into the local izakaya streets, international travelers might face a few "invisible barriers."

- **Otoshi (Appetizer Fee):** A table charge system unique to Japanese izakayas, which comes with a small mandatory appetizer dish.
- **Cash Only:** Credit cards are often not accepted at historic independent shops or street stalls. Always carry some yen!
- **Shrine Etiquette:** Unspoken rules, such as the proper way to purify yourself at the *chozuya* (water pavilion) or avoiding walking perfectly in the center of the torii gates (the path of the gods).

## ✨ You're All Set! Dive Into the Real Heat of Tokyo

While you might feel a bit unsure about cross-cultural rules, there is nothing to fear as long as you know these basic "local rules." A little bit of knowledge and respect will make your trip truly special.

First, take a walk through the quiet forest of Ueno Park and take a deep breath of the majestic, historical air. Then, as dusk approaches, head down to the Ameyoko tracks. Sit shoulder-to-shoulder with the locals under the chaotic, energetic glow of the red lanterns and toast with a beer ("Kanpai!").
