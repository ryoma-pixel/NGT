Pick your Shinjuku Station exit by where you are going, not by the nearest stairs. The East Exit is for Kabukicho, Golden Gai and the big shopping streets; the West Exit is for the skyscrapers and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building; the South and New South exits are for Busta Shinjuku, NEWoMan and the walk to Shinjuku Gyoen. Get that one choice right and most of the station stops being a maze.

Shinjuku is the station that Guinness World Records lists as the busiest in the world. Coming up from the platforms, you meet a river of people, yellow signs pointing in four directions at once, and ticket gates that belong to different railway companies. Locals do not memorise it all. They learn their exit, and a meeting spot they can find with their eyes half closed.

Here is the short version a Shinjuku local would give a friend arriving for the first time.

## ① Why Shinjuku feels like a maze: many stations under one name

**【Several railways, several sets of gates】** "Shinjuku Station" is really a cluster. JR East, Odakyu, Keio, Tokyo Metro and Toei all run trains here, and each has its own platforms and its own ticket gates. An exit that exists for JR may not exist for the subway, and the other way round.

**【Same name, different station】** Seibu-Shinjuku, Shinjuku-sanchome, Nishi-Shinjuku and Higashi-Shinjuku are separate stations nearby. They are not just another exit, so allow extra time when a route tells you to change between them.

**【Construction is part of the scenery】** A long redevelopment of the east and west exit plazas started in July 2021, with work planned to continue for many years. Walkways and signs can move, so trust the current yellow signs over an old screenshot.

## ② The main exits, in one table

The JR exits are the ones most visitors use. Here is what each one is good for.

| JR exit | Where it takes you |
| --- | --- |
| East Exit | Kabukicho, Golden Gai, Shinjuku-dori shopping, Seibu-Shinjuku Station, the east end of the East-West Free Passage |
| Central East Exit | Kabukicho and the east-side shopping streets |
| West Exit | The skyscraper district, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building, Odakyu and Keio lines, the west end of the East-West Free Passage |
| South Exit | Koshu-kaido avenue, Busta Shinjuku |
| New South Exit | Busta Shinjuku, NEWoMan, Takashimaya Times Square, the walk to Shinjuku Gyoen |

**【Up or down?】** Inside the JR area, the East and West exits are generally on the lower level, while the South and New South exits are on the upper level. If you see signs for the wrong pair, you are probably on the wrong floor.

## ③ East Exit: for Kabukicho, Golden Gai and the neon

![The red Kabukicho Ichibangai arch above a busy street](/images/tours/shinjuku-secret-shrine/story-1.webp)

**【The route】** Leave through the East Exit or the Central East Exit, walk north to Yasukuni-dori, and cross. The red Kabukicho Ichibangai arch and the Godzilla Head on the Shinjuku Toho Building appear within a few minutes. Golden Gai and Hanazono Shrine are a little further east.

**【Seibu-Shinjuku】** If you arrive on the Seibu line, you come out at Seibu-Shinjuku Station, which is already on the Kabukicho side and closer to the Godzilla Head than JR.

## ④ West and South: skyscrapers, buses and the park

![Guests with e-scooters in front of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building](/images/tours/shinjuku-escooter-ride/story-2.webp)

**【West Exit】** This side is the business district: office towers, hotels and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building. The Odakyu and Keio lines start from here, as do underground passages that lead towards the towers.

**【South and New South exits】** These open onto Busta Shinjuku, the large highway bus terminal, and the shopping buildings around it. Busta also has a Tokyo Tourist Information Center where you can pick up maps; check the official Go Tokyo site for its current hours. From the New South Exit it is an easy walk to Shinjuku Gyoen.

**【Crossing sides for free】** The East-West Free Passage opened in July 2020. It is about 100 metres long, runs between the JR East and West gates, and links the two sides without passing through ticket gates, so you no longer need a ticket or a long detour to cross.

## ⑤ Meeting spots that are easy to find

The classic answer used to be "in front of Alta", the building with the big screen outside the East Exit. Studio Alta closed in February 2025, so that landmark is no longer a safe bet. These are easier:

1. **A police box (koban) by an exit.** Every Japanese person knows the word koban, and the one outside the JR East Exit is hard to miss.
2. **Busta Shinjuku, under the main sign.** Large, well signed and right by the South Exit.
3. **A named building, at street level.** For example, the entrance of the Shinjuku Toho Building in Kabukicho.

![The Ninja guide pointing to the Busta Shinjuku sign](/images/tours/shinjuku-hanko/meeting.webp)

**【A tip from locals】** Agree on the exit and one visible object, not just "Shinjuku Station". "JR East Exit, by the police box" works. "Shinjuku Station" means hundreds of possible doors.

## ⑥ Five habits that keep you from getting lost

![A shop front on a Shinjuku shopping street](/images/tours/shinjuku-hanko/gallery-7.webp)

1. **Decide your exit before you leave the platform.** Exit signs start on the platform, and the right staircase saves you a long walk.
2. **Follow the yellow exit signs.** Look for the exit name, not just the direction of a street.
3. **Check which railway you are on.** A JR exit is reached through JR gates. If you are on the subway, look for its own exit numbers.
4. **Come up to street level when in doubt.** It is easier to find a landmark outside than in a passage.
5. **Late at night, check before you go.** Some passages and exits close late in the evening, so confirm your route on the station signs if you are heading home after midnight.
