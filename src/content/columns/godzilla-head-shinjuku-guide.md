The Godzilla Head is on the 8th-floor terrace of the Shinjuku Toho Building in Kabukicho, a few minutes' walk from Shinjuku Station. The easiest way to see it is free: from the JR East Exit, cross Yasukuni-dori and look straight up Kabukicho Central Road. The head appears above the street, framed by neon.

The first time you spot it, it feels a little like a film set. Office workers stream past, cinema crowds gather on the pavement, and above all of it a grey monster peers over a building edge as if it has just climbed out of Tokyo Bay. Most people stop, laugh, and take the same photo.

Here is how to find the best view, what the head actually is, and what else is on that street once you have your picture.

## ① What it is: a monster that became a Shinjuku resident

![Guests and the Ninja guide near the Godzilla Head](/images/tours/goldengai-nightlife/gallery-8.webp)

**【The basics】** The head is a life-sized model about 12 metres tall, based on the Godzilla of the early 1990s films. It was unveiled in April 2015, when the Shinjuku Toho Building opened. Toho is the film studio that created Godzilla in 1954.

**【Why here?】** The building stands on the site of the former Shinjuku Koma Theater and Shinjuku Toho Hall, long a symbol of Kabukicho. When it opened, it was the first building over 100 metres tall in the Shinjuku East Exit area. The head sits at roughly the height of the full-sized Godzilla of the early films, so from the street it looks as if the whole monster is standing behind the building.

**【An official resident】** In 2015 Shinjuku ward gave Godzilla a special residency certificate and named it a tourism ambassador, to help bring visitors to Kabukicho.

## ② How to get there without getting lost

Shinjuku Station is famous for its size, so pick your exit before you start walking.

1. **From JR Shinjuku Station**, follow signs to the **East Exit**. Walk to Yasukuni-dori, the wide road north of the station.
2. **Cross Yasukuni-dori** and look for Kabukicho Central Road heading north. The head is visible from here, straight ahead.
3. **Walk up Central Road** for a few minutes. The Shinjuku Toho Building and its cinema entrance are at the end.

From JR Shinjuku Station it is about a 9-minute walk. **Seibu-Shinjuku Station** is closer, about 3 minutes on foot, if you are coming on the Seibu line.

**【Look down too】** Near the entrance of the building there are Godzilla manhole covers set into the pavement, an easy second photo that many people walk right over.

## ③ The best free views, and the terrace question

![The red Kabukicho Ichibangai arch above a busy street](/images/tours/shinjuku-secret-shrine/story-1.webp)

**【Free views from the street】**

- **Yasukuni-dori, at the foot of Central Road.** The classic shot: the head straight ahead, above the street.
- **Central Road itself.** As you walk closer, the head looms higher and the cinema signs fill the lower half of your frame.
- **In front of the building.** Step back across the small square and tilt your phone up for the dramatic low angle.

**【Getting close on the terrace】** The head stands on an outdoor terrace connected to the Hotel Gracery Shinjuku, which occupies the upper floors. In the past, hotel guests and customers of the hotel's café could go out on the terrace. Several travel guides reported that access was closed for a time for safety reasons, and the rules have changed more than once. Check the hotel's official site or ask at the front desk before you plan around it.

**【Staying the night】** The hotel offers Godzilla-themed rooms and rooms with a view of the head. Room types change, so check the hotel's official booking page.

## ④ When it roars: timing your visit

The head is not just a statue. At set times its eyes light up, it roars, and smoke drifts around it. Travel guides list different schedules for this show, and some say the times change with the season, so there is no single reliable timetable we can give you. Ask the hotel or check its official site on the day.

**【Day or night?】**

| Time | What you get |
|---|---|
| Daytime | Clear detail on the head, fewer crowds on the pavement |
| Early evening | Neon switches on, the head is lit, the street is lively |
| Later at night | Most dramatic photos, busier and noisier streets |

For a first visit, early evening is the sweet spot: the neon is on, but the streets are full of people heading to dinner and films.

## ⑤ What else is on that street

You are standing in the middle of Kabukicho, so there is plenty within a short walk.

- **Toho Cinemas Shinjuku**, inside the same building, one of the largest cinema complexes in Tokyo.
- **Tokyu Kabukicho Tower**, the tall entertainment tower that opened nearby in 2023, with cinemas, a hotel and a food hall.
- **Kabukicho Ichibangai**, the main street, entered under its lit red gate from Yasukuni-dori.
- **Hidden shrines** tucked between buildings, with small torii gates you would never guess were there.

![A Ninja guide with a lantern in a tunnel of red torii gates](/images/tours/shinjuku-secret-shrine/story-2.webp)

**【A quieter side】** A short walk east, Golden Gai's narrow lanes of tiny bars and the grounds of Hanazono Shrine feel like a different city from the neon of Central Road.

![The glowing Golden Gai sign at the entrance to the alleys at night](/images/tours/goldengai-nightlife/story-1.webp)![Colorful bar signboards crowding a narrow Golden Gai alley](/images/tours/goldengai-nightlife/story-2.webp)

## ⑥ Three tips for a relaxed visit

1. **Ignore street offers.** Kabukicho has touts who offer cheap bars or special deals. Smile, say "No, thank you" and keep walking. Choose places yourself.
2. **Stay on the main, lit streets** if you are with children, and visit early in the evening.
3. **Note your way back.** Remember whether you came from the JR East Exit or Seibu-Shinjuku Station, and check your last train if you stay late.
